Workout rules

The principles encoded into the offline generator.

01

The workout serves the day

Generate the session requested in the moment. Do not force a rigid weekly split or substitute a different body part because a theoretical schedule says so. Consistency, enjoyment, joint comfort and repeated quality exposure come first.

02

Two quality sets

Normal strength and hypertrophy exercises use two working sets. Warm-up sets do not count.

  • Set 1: a challenging quality set, normally 1-3 reps in reserve.
  • Set 2: match the load or use a controlled back-off to preserve technique and useful reps.
  • Swings, carries, complexes, mace, mobility and conditioning may use rounds, reps, time or distance.
03

Session construction

Most workouts contain four to seven exercises and last 30-50 minutes unless another duration is chosen. Use one or two primary movements, two or three supporting movements, then optional isolation, carries, core, mobility or shoulder-health work. Supersets are used when they save time without harming performance.

04

Effort and reps

Heavy compoundsUsually 5-8 reps

General hypertrophyUsually 6-12 reps

IsolationUsually 10-20 reps

Failure is optional, not the default. Avoid repeated grinding compounds. Effort, control and joint comfort matter more than an exact rep target.

05

Exercise selection

Match the requested session, location, equipment, energy, recent activity and pain. Prefer familiar movements that Steve enjoys, especially stable machines, cables, dumbbells and kettlebells suited to hard two-set training. Replace irritating exercises rather than forcing them; consider neutral grips, rings, dumbbells, kettlebells and cables for more comfortable positions.

06

Session patterns

PushChest, comfortable shoulder press, triceps, optional delts

PullVertical pull, row, upper back, elbow-friendly biceps, optional carry

LegsKnee pattern, hinge, hamstrings, glutes, optional calves

Upper1-2 pushes, 1-2 pulls, shoulders or upper back, optional arms

LowerKnee pattern, hinge or hamstrings, unilateral work, optional calves/core

OtherFull body, bodyweight, kettlebell, mobility and mixed sessions follow the same low-friction philosophy

07

Home / garage

Equipment: 1 x 12 kg; pairs of 20, 24 and 32 kg kettlebells; pull-up bar; gymnastic rings; parallettes; resistance bands; 7.5 kg mace.

Use this equipment for athletic, movement-based and low-friction sessions: presses, rows, squats, RDLs, swings, cleans, carries, pull-ups, ring work, get-ups, bands, mace 360s and rotational work. Complexes are welcome when they make training simpler and more enjoyable.

08

Kongs

Use familiar, straightforward hypertrophy work. Highest priorities include iso-lateral chest press, incline dumbbell press, dips when comfortable, lat pulldowns, iso-lateral high row, seated cable row, face pulls, cable or machine lateral raises, elbow-friendly curls, cable triceps work, leg press, leg extensions, hamstring curls, dumbbell RDLs and standing calf raises.

Prefer dumbbells, machines and dips over barbell pressing when shoulder or elbow comfort matters. Leg press is the default heavy quad choice; trap-bar deadlifts are optional, not mandatory.

09

Hotel and bodyweight

Hotel workouts use only the equipment selected. With no hotel equipment, automatically use bodyweight. Keep sessions simple. When load is limited, use range of motion, tempo, pauses, unilateral variations and mechanical drop sets to make two hard sets productive.

10

Warm-up and progression

Warm-ups are normally 5-10 minutes and never another workout. Progress conventional movements with more reps, more load, or better technique and range. Progress kettlebells, rings and bodyweight with harder variations, tempo, pauses, range or improved density. Not every workout must beat the last one.
